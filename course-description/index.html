    FanPedia: An Intelligent Textbook on Fans & Blowers¶

    Title: FanPedia: An Intelligent Textbook on Fans & Blowers

    Target Audience: Adult professional development: new application engineers, inside and outside sales staff, manufacturer's representatives, distributors, specifying engineers, and plant or facilities staff who need to identify, specify, or order industrial and commercial fans.

    Prerequisites:

    • High-school algebra (ratios, square and cube relationships, reading a graph)
    • Basic physical units: feet, inches, pounds, horsepower, RPM, degrees Fahrenheit
    • No prior fan, HVAC, or mechanical engineering coursework is assumed

    Course Overview¶

    Fans move the air in nearly every building, factory, laboratory, and process plant, yet the vocabulary used to describe them is dense and unforgiving. A single order has to state the fan type, the arrangement, the impeller design, the rotation and discharge, the motor position, the construction class, and a list of accessories. Getting any one of those wrong means a fan that does not fit, does not perform, or cannot be installed. This course teaches that vocabulary and the engineering reasons behind it, so learners can read a catalog, understand a specification, and describe a fan precisely.

    The course is built from Twin City Fan's FanPedia: The Fan & Blower Encyclopedia (the online edition at tcf.com/fanpedia and the PDF edition) and from Twin City Fan's current product catalogs on tcf.com. FanPedia supplies the structure: fan basics, fan components, fan construction, accessories, and technical descriptions. The catalogs supply the current product families and their performance data. Where a current catalog and FanPedia disagree, the catalog is treated as authoritative.

    Each chapter moves from recognizing a part or configuration, to explaining why it exists, to choosing it for a given application. Interactive MicroSims let learners rotate impellers, change arrangements, and watch how speed changes airflow, pressure, and power. The course ends with a capstone in which learners produce a complete fan specification for a realistic application.

    Main Topics Covered¶

    1. Fan families and product lines: housed centrifugal, plenum and plug, inline centrifugal, axial (tubeaxial and vaneaxial), mixed flow, radial bladed, radial tip, and pressure blowers; and the application-specific lines in the current catalogs: roof ventilators, centrifugal roof and wall exhausters, wall-mounted fans, laboratory fume exhaust, kitchen and restaurant exhaust, smoke and heat exhaust, gravity relief ventilators, ceiling and cabinet ventilators, filtered supply fans, utility sets, fiberglass fans, mancoolers, and air kits; commercial versus industrial duty
    2. Fan anatomy: exploded views of each fan family and the names of every major part
    3. Fan arrangements: the standard drive arrangements (1, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9, 10) and their plug (P), vertical (V), clamshell (CS), and swingout (SO) variants; overhung versus center-hung impellers; direct versus belt drive
    4. Impellers: centrifugal impeller types (backward inclined, airfoil, forward curved, radial bladed, radial tip, mixed flow) and axial impeller types; impeller orientation, airflow path, and rotation; hub types (straight bore, taper lock)
    5. Discharge, rotation, and motor position: designating rotation (clockwise / counterclockwise) and discharge angle, standard motor positions, and the restrictions discharge places on motor position
    6. Housing construction: housing styles, welded versus bolted construction, split housings (horizontal, three-way, pie), and access provisions
    7. Drive train components: induction and electronically commutated (EC) motors, motor bases and slide bases, motor positioners, shafts, couplings, pedestals, and shaft coolers
    8. Bearings and lubrication: bearing types, split roller and flange bearings, oil mist and static oil lubrication, bearing positioners and stop blocks, and vibration sensors
    9. Inlets and outlets: inlet funnels and venturis, inlet boxes (integral, bolt-on, free-standing), evasés, inlet and outlet flex connectors, companion flanges, and inlet silencers
    10. Flow control: inlet vanes (nested and external), outlet and inlet box dampers (parallel and opposed blade), gravity dampers, manual and motorized dampers, and blast gates and slide gates
    11. Special construction: spark-resistant construction Types A, B, and C; high-temperature construction and shaft cooling; insulated fans; nominally leak-tight, RTO (regenerative thermal oxidizer), and high-moisture service; swingout and clamshell designs
    12. Installation and support accessories: unitary, isolation, and concrete inertia bases; vibration isolation (floor mounted, ceiling hung, seismic, restrained); drains and weep holes; shaft seals; grounding devices; anti-backspin devices; and piezometer rings for airflow measurement
    13. Fan performance fundamentals for reading catalogs: airflow (CFM), static and total pressure, brake horsepower, efficiency, fan curves and system curves, the operating point, the fan laws, air density and temperature corrections, and sound ratings
    14. Catalogs, technical descriptions, and specifications: reading a current Twin City Fan catalog and performance table, reading FanPedia technical descriptions, and assembling a complete fan description for an order or specification

    Topics Not Covered¶

    • Aerodynamic design of impellers or housings, including computational fluid dynamics
    • Duct system design and full HVAC load calculations (system curves are covered only as far as needed to find an operating point)
    • Electrical design of motors and the programming of variable frequency drives
    • Detailed field service, repair, balancing, and maintenance procedures
    • Pricing, quoting, lead times, and order-entry systems
    • Fan manufacturing processes and shop practices
    • Laboratory test procedures for certifying fan ratings
    • Products from manufacturers other than Twin City Fan

    Learning Outcomes¶

    After completing this course, students will be able to:

    Remember¶

    Retrieving, recognizing, and recalling relevant knowledge from long-term memory.

    • List the major fan families (centrifugal, plenum and plug, inline centrifugal, axial, mixed flow, radial bladed, pressure blower) and name an example product line for each
    • Recall the standard fan arrangement numbers and identify which are direct drive and which are belt drive
    • Name the centrifugal impeller types and the hub types
    • Identify the major parts of a fan from an exploded view: housing, impeller, inlet funnel, shaft, bearings, pedestal, and motor base
    • Define CFM, static pressure, total pressure, brake horsepower, and fan efficiency
    • Recall the three spark-resistant construction types (A, B, C)
    • List common fan accessories, such as dampers, inlet vanes, inlet boxes, evasés, drains, shaft seals, and isolation bases

    Understand¶

    Constructing meaning from instructional messages, including oral, written, and graphic communication.

    • Explain how air moves through a centrifugal impeller compared with an axial or mixed-flow impeller
    • Describe how an arrangement determines where the motor, bearings, and impeller sit relative to one another
    • Explain how rotation and discharge are designated, viewed from the drive side
    • Interpret a fan curve and explain why a fan operates where its curve crosses the system curve
    • Explain the fan laws: how airflow, pressure, and power change with speed and impeller diameter
    • Explain why air density and temperature change catalog performance, and why high-temperature service needs special construction
    • Summarize what each spark-resistant construction type does and does not protect against

    Apply¶

    Carrying out or using a procedure in a given situation.

    • Use the fan laws to estimate the new airflow, pressure, and horsepower after a speed change
    • Apply density and temperature corrections to a catalog rating
    • Read a current catalog performance table to find the RPM and brake horsepower for a required airflow and static pressure
    • Write the complete rotation and discharge designation for a fan shown in a drawing
    • Determine which motor positions are allowed for a given discharge
    • Choose the arrangement that keeps the motor and bearings out of a hot or corrosive airstream

    Analyze¶

    Breaking material into constituent parts and determining how the parts relate to one another and to an overall structure or purpose.

    • Compare impeller types on efficiency, pressure capability, tolerance for dust or material in the airstream, and noise
    • Break down a full fan description into its parts (type, size, arrangement, rotation, discharge, motor position, construction class, accessories) and check that they are consistent
    • Distinguish between FanPedia guidance and current catalog data, and identify which source governs when they differ
    • Diagnose whether a fan is mismatched to its system from its operating point on the fan and system curves
    • Contrast the flow-control options (inlet vanes, outlet dampers, speed control) by efficiency and turndown

    Evaluate¶

    Making judgments based on criteria and standards through checking and critiquing.

    • Judge which fan family best suits an application, given airflow, pressure, temperature, airstream contents, space, and noise limits
    • Assess whether a proposed fan specification is complete and internally consistent, and justify each correction
    • Evaluate whether an application needs special construction (spark resistant, high temperature, leak tight, high moisture) and which type
    • Critique a proposed accessory list for missing items, such as drains for wet service or shaft seals for leak-tight service
    • Recommend direct drive or belt drive for an application and defend the choice

    Create¶

    Putting elements together to form a coherent or functional whole; reorganizing elements into a new pattern or structure.

    • Write a complete fan specification for a described application, from fan type through accessories
    • Build a selection checklist that a sales or application engineer could use to gather every value needed for a fan order
    • Design a comparison of two or three candidate fans for one duty point, with performance, construction, and accessory trade-offs
    • Produce an annotated drawing that shows a fan's arrangement, rotation, discharge, and motor position
    • Capstone project: given a realistic industrial application (for example, a hot process exhaust with a dusty airstream and a fixed footprint), select the fan family, arrangement, impeller, construction, and accessories from the current catalog, apply the needed performance corrections, and present a complete written specification with the reasoning for each choice
