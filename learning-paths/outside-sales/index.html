    Outside Sales and Manufacturer's Representatives¶

    This path is for you if you work with customers in the field and need to match a job to the right Twin City Fan product line, then explain why. It teaches the fan families and application lines, how air moves through each impeller type, how to read a fan curve and an operating point, and how to discuss drive, construction and accessory options and the trade-offs between candidate fans.

    The path leans on the product-family, performance and selection chapters and only skims the detailed hardware chapters. Follow the checklist in order, and spend your sim time on the family sorter and product-line matcher: matching a described job to a product is the closest thing in the book to a customer conversation.

    Target Concepts¶

    These are the concepts that define the job outcome for this path. Everything else on the path is a prerequisite of one of them in the learning graph.

    Concept Taught in Why it is on this path
    Fan Family Ch. 4: Fan Families Remember: list the major fan families.
    Commercial Duty Fan Ch. 4: Commercial and Industrial Duty Topic 1: commercial versus industrial duty.
    Industrial Duty Fan Ch. 4: Commercial and Industrial Duty Topic 1: commercial versus industrial duty.
    Power Roof Ventilator Ch. 4: Power Roof Ventilators Topic 1 and Remember: name an example product line (roof ventilators and exhausters) for each family.
    Laboratory Fume Exhaust Fan Ch. 4: Laboratory Fume Exhaust Fans Topic 1: the application-specific lines in the current catalogs (laboratory fume exhaust).
    Plug Fan Ch. 6: Plug Fans: The Impeller Goes In, the Drive Stays Out Remember: plenum and plug fans are a major family, the usual air-handler product.
    Impeller Airflow Path Ch. 9: Impeller Airflow Path Understand: explain how air moves through a centrifugal impeller compared with an axial or mixed-flow impeller.
    Operating Point Ch. 10: System Resistance, the System Curve and the Operating Point (opening) Understand: interpret a fan curve and explain why a fan operates where its curve crosses the system curve.
    Fan Family Selection Ch. 26: Fan Family Selection Evaluate: judge which fan family best suits an application, given airflow, pressure, temperature, airstream contents, space and noise limits.
    Impeller Selection Ch. 27: Impeller Selection Analyze: compare impeller types on efficiency, pressure capability, dust tolerance and noise.
    Drive Selection Ch. 27: Drive Selection Evaluate: recommend direct drive or belt drive for an application and defend the choice.
    Construction Selection Ch. 27: Construction Selection Evaluate: whether an application needs special construction (spark resistant, high temperature, leak tight, high moisture) and which type.
    Product Options Ch. 26: Product Options Remember: list common fan accessories; options are part of every customer conversation.
    Application Requirements Ch. 27: Application Requirements (opening) Create: build a selection checklist a sales engineer can use to gather every value needed for a fan order.
    Selection Trade-Offs Ch. 27: Selection Trade-Offs Create: compare two or three candidate fans for one duty point, with performance, construction and accessory trade-offs.

    When You Finish¶

    Put the whole path to work with the Fan Selection Workflow, a twelve-step selection flowchart that checks which step uses each piece of job information, then walk a real inquiry through those steps before your next customer call. If you write or review specifications with consulting engineers, continue with the Specifying, Consulting and Application Engineers path for the fan laws, system effect and specification writing.
