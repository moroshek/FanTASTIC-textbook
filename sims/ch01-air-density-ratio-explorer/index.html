    Air Density Ratio Explorer¶

    Run the Air Density Ratio Explorer MicroSim Fullscreen

    About This MicroSim¶

    Fan catalogs are printed at standard air: 70°F at sea level, 0.075 lb/ft³. Real jobs are hotter, colder or higher. The density ratio (DR) is the actual density divided by standard density:

    \[ DR = \frac{530}{T + 460} \times \frac{P_b}{29.92}, \qquad \frac{P_b}{29.92} = (1 - 6.8754 \times 10^{-6} \times h)^{5.2559} \]

    where \( T \) is the air temperature in °F and \( h \) is the site altitude in feet. At a fixed speed a fan moves the same CFM in thin air or thick air, while its static pressure and brake horsepower scale with DR. That gives two rules: to predict what a fan really develops at the job, multiply the catalog value by DR; to select from a standard-air catalog, divide the required actual pressure by DR.

    The chart plots DR against air temperature from −20°F to 600°F for four site altitudes (0, 2,000, 5,000 and 8,000 ft). The temperature and altitude sliders move a marker (✕) to any setting. A reference panel lists the altitude pressure ratios from 0 to 10,000 ft.

    Four challenges, in this order, ask for six entries:

    # Situation Asked Model answer
    1 250°F at sea level; catalog shows 4.0 in. wg at standard air Density ratio; actual SP 0.746; 2.99 in. wg
    2 70°F at 5,000 ft; job needs 2.0 in. wg actual Density ratio; catalog SP to select at 0.832; 2.40 in. wg
    3 120°F at 5,000 ft Density ratio 0.760
    4 600°F at sea level Density ratio 0.500

    A ratio is correct within ±0.005 and a pressure within ±0.03 in. wg of the unrounded model value. Each entry has two attempts. For challenges 1 and 2 you type both boxes and press Check; the pressure is checked once the ratio is right or revealed. A correct entry shows "✓ Correct: ." A first wrong attempt names the rule to use (and the slip, if the sim recognizes it: °F used directly, the 460 left out, multiplying where you must divide, one factor of two). A second wrong attempt shows the model answer with the challenge's calculation, and the entry counts as missed. An empty or non-numeric entry gets "Enter a number" and does not use an attempt. The running score reads "Entries correct: n of 6". Mastery is all six entries correct within two attempts each.

    During the challenges the chart shows the shape of the curves and the marker's position, but no numeric ratio: read the curve against the axis to sanity-check your calculation. After challenge 4, Explore mode (never scored) unlocks the exact readouts. Hovering the chart shows DR to 3 decimals and density to 4 decimals for each altitude, and the marker readout shows the temperature factor, the pressure ratio, the density and what a fan rated 4.00 in. wg at standard air develops there, at the same CFM and RPM.

    How to Use¶

    1. Read challenge 1. Move the Air temperature and Site altitude sliders so the marker sits at the stated condition, and note roughly where it falls on the axis.
    2. Calculate the density ratio (and, for challenges 1 and 2, the corrected pressure). Type the answers and press Check (or Enter).
    3. Read the feedback. If you have an attempt left, fix the step it names and check again.
    4. Press Next challenge. After challenge 4, read the summary.
    5. In Explore mode, hover the curves and move the marker. Compare 250°F at sea level with 70°F at 8,000 ft: two very different jobs with nearly the same density. Press Start over to repeat the challenges.

    Keyboard: Tab reaches every control. Enter in an answer box checks it; after an entry is settled, Enter goes to the next challenge. The arrow keys move a focused slider.

    Data label: "Curves from DR = 530 ÷ (T + 460) × (1 − 6.8754 × 10⁻⁶ × h)^5.2559 · Catalog pressures in challenges 1 and 2 are illustrative values", shown under the chart.

    Lesson Plan¶

    Learning Objective¶

    The learner will calculate the density ratio for a stated air temperature and site altitude, and use it to correct a static pressure between catalog and job conditions, to within ±0.005 on the ratio and ±0.03 in. wg on the pressure (Bloom: Apply, verb: calculate).

    Audience¶

    Adult professional development: new application engineers, sales staff, representatives and facilities staff.

    Duration¶

    10-15 minutes

    Prerequisites¶

    • Air density and standard air (70°F, sea level, 0.075 lb/ft³)
    • Density ratio, air temperature, altitude and absolute pressure
    • Static pressure
    • The two correction rules: multiply to predict, divide to select

    All are covered in the Chapter 1 sections "Air Density", "Air Temperature" and "Altitude".

    Activities¶

    1. Challenges (8 min): Place the marker first, then calculate and check each entry. Two attempts each.
    2. Explore (4 min): Hover the curves at 250°F and 600°F. Then find an altitude where 70°F air is as thin as 120°F air at sea level.
    3. Discussion (3 min): A fan selected for a 5,000 ft site straight from the catalog at 2.0 in. wg: is it short of pressure or oversized, and by how much?

    Common Misconceptions Addressed¶

    • Hot air or high altitude reduces the CFM a fan moves at a given speed: the Explore readout states that CFM and RPM stay the same while pressure and BHP scale with DR.
    • The temperature correction can be done in °F directly (70 ÷ 250): challenge 1 recognizes 0.28 and points to absolute temperature; challenge 4 recognizes a missing 460.
    • To select a fan for thin air you multiply the required pressure by DR: challenge 2 recognizes the multiplied answer and explains that it would undersize the fan.

    Assessment¶

    • Running score shown as "Entries correct: n of 6"; mastery is 6 of 6 entries correct within two attempts each. The summary shows "Mastery reached (6 of 6 needed)." or what is still needed, and lists any missed entry with its calculation.
    • Explore mode is not evidence of mastery. Follow up: a motor sized only for the hot running condition of challenge 1 starts up cold. What happens to its load?
